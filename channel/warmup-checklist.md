# The Last Lantern — Channel Warm-Up Checklist (legitimate version)

This is about giving YouTube's trust systems a real signal that you're an actual channel with a consistent identity — not about gaming anything. Use your own phone/account normally throughout; don't switch devices or hop networks mid-process, that itself reads as suspicious.

## Days 1–3 (before any upload)
- [ ] Complete the FULL channel profile: banner, avatar, description, links — an empty profile pushing a first video looks like spam.
- [ ] Verify the channel with a phone number in YouTube Studio (unlocks features, genuine trust signal).
- [ ] Watch and genuinely engage with 10–15 videos in-niche (mystery/history/true-story channels) — real watch time, a few thoughtful comments, subscribe to 5–10 channels you actually like.
- [ ] Create 1–2 playlists on the channel (even empty, to fill later) around sub-pillar names — helps YouTube assign a clear topic label early.

## Days 4–5
- [ ] Upload the **strongest** episode first — YouTube weighs early uploads heavily when deciding how much to invest in distributing what comes next.
- [ ] Do NOT upload multiple videos in a single burst on day one — one strong upload, then hold to the weekly cadence.

## Ongoing
- [ ] Stick to the weekly schedule consistently — irregular gaps or sudden bursts both read as untrustworthy.
- [ ] Never delete and re-upload an underperforming video — known negative signal. Leave it up, let the channel's overall trend speak.
- [ ] Reply to comments genuinely — this is real engagement, not a trick, and one of the few unambiguous trust signals fully in your control.

## Consistency ("fingerprint") rules — the legitimate version
What actually matters: not looking like a template-only, mass-produced pipeline.
- **Vary the intro, not just the topic.** Same logo sting + same music sting + identical opening line structure every episode is a detectable template pattern. Vary phrasing/pacing of the opening line even while reusing the same hook *techniques*.
- **Don't reuse identical thumbnail layouts pixel-for-pixel.** Consistent *style* (see thumbnail-style-guide.md) is good branding; identical *layout* every time reads as templated.
- **Real script variance is the biggest one.** Two episodes about two different places should not follow an identical sentence-by-sentence skeleton with nouns swapped. This is both a policy-safety measure (literally what "mass-produced/repetitious" checks for) and what keeps an audience.
- **Metadata:** write a genuine, specific 2–3 sentence description per episode — don't copy-paste a template with only the title swapped.
- **Upload timing:** consistent day/time, but normal human variance (same day, different hour) is fine and more natural than robotic exact-second scheduling.
