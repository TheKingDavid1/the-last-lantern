# The Last Lantern — Creative Brief for Collaborators
*For: video creator / editor and graphics designer. Read this before starting any asset work.*

## 1. What this channel is
**The Last Lantern** is a YouTube channel of calm, narrated true stories — think premium documentary, not viral clickbait. It's a faceless channel: there is no on-camera host, only narration over atmospheric imagery. The narrator persona is called **"the Keeper"** — an anonymous, consistent voice who "tends" the channel's lantern, pulling one forgotten story into the light each week.

**Four content types (sub-pillars), rotated week to week:**
1. **Unsolved disappearances** — ships, expeditions, people who vanished without explanation
2. **Eerie/forgotten history** — strange historical events with an unsettling edge
3. **Abandoned places** — real locations with a documented backstory
4. **Sleep-story retellings** — slower, dimmer, longer-paused versions of the above, explicitly meant to be fallen asleep to

**Format:** one 10–18 minute long-form video per week, plus 4–6 short vertical clips cut from each episode.

## 2. The tone — read this twice, it's the most important part
**Calm and cinematic, never hype.** This is the single biggest thing to get right. The channel's whole appeal is that it feels like a slow-paced, high-production documentary — think LEMMiNO, Wendigoon, Amy's Crypt, Bedtime Stories as reference points, not MrBeast-style fast-cut energy or tabloid true-crime.

**What this means concretely:**
- Long, held shots are a *feature*, not something to rush through — especially in sleep-story episodes
- No jump-scares, no sudden volume spikes, no hyper-fast cutting
- Narration is delivered flat and calm even on the biggest reveal — restraint reads as more credible in this niche, excitement reads as less credible
- Fact and theory are always kept visibly separate — this audience distrusts creators who blur the two

## 3. Visual style
- **Look:** painterly/cinematic digital illustration style, high detail, moody lighting, dark base tones with warm amber highlights. Think "movie poster," not flat AI-art or stock-photo look.
- **Motion (for the video creator):** currently, all footage is static images with slow camera pans/zooms (Ken Burns style) — no actual video generation yet. If adding real AI-generated motion clips going forward:
  - **Image-to-video only, from an already-approved still** — never blind text-to-video for anything in the final cut. This anchors the generated motion to composition/lighting we've already signed off on.
  - **Maximum 2–3 motion clips per episode**, reserved only for the cold-open hook and the emotional climax — never for establishing/overview shots.
  - Every generated clip needs to be checked at start/middle/end for warping or lighting inconsistency before it goes in the final cut. If anything looks off, it gets swapped for a different still — never masked in editing.
  - Most video-gen tools produce short clips (roughly 4–8 seconds) — expect motion to cover the *first part* of a moment, with the shot settling back into a still pan for the remainder, not a full swap of a long held shot.

## 4. Color system (critical for the graphics designer)
One dark base (black/near-black) + exactly **one** accent color per thumbnail/episode, tied to which sub-pillar the episode belongs to:

| Sub-pillar | Accent color | Typical "tension object" |
|---|---|---|
| Disappearances | Cold blue-black | Empty chair, an object left behind, a shadowed figure walking away |
| Eerie/forgotten history | Deep red-black | Close-up historical document/artifact, single-source dramatic lighting |
| Abandoned places | Forest green-black | A single lit window in a dark structure, an overgrown doorway |
| Sleep-story retellings | Muted purple-black | Softer focus, one small light source, more negative space than any other category |

## 5. Thumbnail rules
- **One clear focal point per thumbnail** — never a busy/cluttered scene
- **One accent color only** (see table above) — no rainbow palettes
- **~60% dark negative space, ~40% lit subject** — restraint outperforms clutter specifically in this niche
- **3–5 words of title text max**, a fragment of the hook, not the full title
- **No "SHOCKING" tabloid-red text style** — wrong register for this brand
- **Logo watermark: same corner, same size, every single time** — this is the one thing that should be pixel-identical across episodes
- Typography: bold condensed sans (we use Anton for headlines, Poppins ExtraBold for subtext)
- Someone should be able to glance at a thumbnail for one second and know which sub-pillar it belongs to, without reading the title

## 6. Video structure template (for pacing reference)
1. **Cold open / hook (first 3–5 sec):** the core stakes/anomaly stated immediately — no channel branding, no throat-clearing, no slow poetic build-up before this line lands
2. **Setup (next ~2 min):** ordinary context — who, where, when — paced slower since tension is already banked
3. **Escalation (up to ~70% mark):** sequential reveals, each raising the strangeness
4. **Climax (~70–85%):** the single strangest fact, delivered with the *most* restraint
5. **Resolution (~85–95%):** explicitly separate what's known from what's theorized
6. **Closing beat (final 5%):** quiet, atmospheric, often returns to the opening image/line — slows down further on sleep-story episodes specifically

## 7. Branding assets that already exist
- **Logo:** dark circular emblem, a lit oil lantern, amber flame against a dark navy/teal background
- **Outro:** animated pulsing "SUBSCRIBE" button + a shaking bell icon — this motion is intentional, keep it if redesigning
- **Captions:** karaoke-style — a short phrase stays on screen, with the currently-spoken word lighting up amber as it's said (not one word flashing at a time)

## 8. Audio
- Calm, TTS narration (the Keeper's voice), minimal vocal inflection even at big reveals
- Soft procedurally-generated ambient bed underneath (wind/low drone) — kept quiet, never competing with narration clarity
