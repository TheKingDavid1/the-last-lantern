# Research Prompt for Claude — Hooks, Topics & Style Playbook for "The Last Lantern"

Paste this directly into Claude:

---

I run a YouTube channel called **The Last Lantern** — calm, narrated true stories (unsolved disappearances, forgotten/eerie history, abandoned places, and slower "sleep story" retellings), told by an anonymous consistent narrator persona called "the Keeper." The channel is faceless (no on-camera host), TTS/recorded narration over atmospheric still imagery with slow pans, positioned as premium/cinematic rather than clickbait-y. Videos are 10-18 min long-form plus 4-6 Shorts cut from each episode.

I need you to research and build a practical playbook from channels that are actually succeeding in this exact space — examples to study: **LEMMiNO, Nexpo, Wendigoon, Lazy Masquerade, Bedtime Stories, Amy's Crypt, Scary Interesting, Dark5, MrBallen** — plus any other channel you find that fits (calm/authoritative narration, faceless or mostly faceless, mystery/history/true-story format, 500K+ subscribers).

Please produce:

## 1. Hook Library
Pull or closely paraphrase 15-20 actual opening lines/hooks from top videos in this space. For each, note:
- The exact hook line or a close paraphrase
- Why it works (what specific technique: stated stakes, direct address, ticking-clock framing, contradiction/irony, etc.)
- How many seconds into the video the "payoff" or stakes statement lands

Then write 10 NEW hook lines in the same style, usable as templates for our upcoming episodes (topics already in our queue: SS Baychimo ghost ship, Bannerman Castle, the Dancing Plague of 1518, Mary Celeste, Winchester Mystery House, Hashima Island, the Great Emu War, D.B. Cooper, Roanoke, Craco Italy).

## 2. Structure & Pacing Playbook
For 3-5 of the channels above, break down:
- How long from video start to the first "hook payoff" (the moment the core stakes/mystery is stated)
- The overall structure after that (how do they sequence setup → escalation → climax → resolution)
- Where they use pacing shifts (fast vs. slow sections) and why
- Anything about narration voice/tone that stands out (pacing of speech, use of pauses, rhetorical questions, direct address to viewer)

## 3. Title & Thumbnail Patterns
List 15-20 actual high-performing titles from this niche. Identify the recurring formulas (e.g., "[Place]: [Ironic outcome]", "[Number] + [strange event]", direct question hooks). Do the same for thumbnail composition patterns specific to this niche (not generic YouTube advice — what specifically works for mystery/history/abandoned-place content).

## 4. Topic Radar
Beyond our current 10-topic queue, suggest 15 more real, well-documented topics that fit our four sub-pillars (unsolved disappearances / eerie forgotten history / abandoned places / slow sleep-story retellings), prioritizing:
- Topics currently under-covered by the big channels (less oversaturated = easier to rank/differentiate)
- A mix of low-risk (no identifiable real-tragedy victims) and medium-risk topics, matching our existing risk-rating system
- Note your confidence in factual documentation quality for each (we require a fact-check pass before scripting)

## 5. What NOT to Copy
Flag anything you notice these channels do that would be a bad fit for a *calm/sleep-positioned* channel specifically (e.g., jump-scare sound design, hyper-fast cutting, sensationalized thumbnails) — we want the hook/structure lessons, not a pacing overhaul that would undercut our actual product.
