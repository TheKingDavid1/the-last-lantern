## Upload sequence & schedule (optimized for hitting Expanded tier fastest)

### Why Shorts carry the monetization math here
Both thresholds (Expanded: 500 subs + 3M Shorts views/90d OR 3,000 watch hrs; Full YPP: 1,000 subs + 10M Shorts views/90d OR 4,000 watch hrs) only need **one** of the two paths satisfied, not both. Calm, slow narration long-form is a weak fit for racking up 4,000 raw watch hours fast on a brand-new channel — but it's an excellent hook-supplier for high-volume Shorts. **Shorts views are almost certainly your faster path to both thresholds.** Long-form still matters (it's the credibility/authorship anchor and where actual watch-hour progress accrues over time), but don't wait on it to carry the threshold math.

### First-week launch sequence (channel "warm-up")
Don't publish the long-form and go silent for a week — warm the channel up first:

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Day −2 | Post **Short #1** (X-ray/carrier-pigeon hook) — no long-form live yet. Caption: "Full story drops Friday 🕯️" |
| Day −1 | Post **Short #2** (coffee-cups-on-the-table hook) |
| **Day 0 (launch)** | Publish the **full long-form Episode 1**. Pin a comment: "This is the first story on the channel — more every week." |
| Day 0 + a few hours | Post **Short #3** (garden-in-the-desert hook), caption pointing to the full episode |
| Day +1 | Post **Short #4** (hyenas-moved-in hook) |

This gives the algorithm 2 small, low-risk signals (Shorts) before the big swing (long-form), and gives the long-form a small head start of subscribers already primed to see it land.

### Ongoing weekly rhythm (Weeks 2–8, unchanged in structure from the original plan)
- 1 long-form on the same day/time every week (pick one day and don't move it — consistency is a stronger signal than the "perfect" day)
- 4–6 Shorts spread across the week, not clustered — post roughly every 1–2 days, at least one the same day as the long-form
- Recommended anchor day: **Friday or Saturday** for the long-form (relaxation/sleep content consumption tends to rise into the weekend)

### What time to post (this part matters more than people think)
You're in Lagos/Yola time (WAT, UTC+1). For this niche, the two audiences worth optimizing for are US viewers (highest RPM) and UK/Western Europe viewers (good RPM, and conveniently your timezone twin).

- **WAT is the same clock time as UK summer time (BST, UTC+1).** So an 8–10 PM WAT upload lands at 8–10 PM in the UK — directly inside UK evening prime time.
- That same 8–10 PM WAT upload lands at **3–5 PM US Eastern** — a few hours *before* the 7–9 PM US Eastern evening peak. That gap is intentional: publishing a little ahead of the peak gives YouTube's algorithm time to test the video on early viewers before the big US evening wave arrives, instead of publishing cold right into it.

**Recommendation: publish between 8:00–10:00 PM WAT.** Do this for both the long-form and the "anchor" Short of the week. Spread the rest of the week's Shorts at varied times (e.g., late morning WAT ~11 AM–1 PM, which catches US East Coast commute/lunch scrolling) so you're not only ever showing up in one timezone's feed.

## Targeting US/foreign audiences for better RPM — what actually works (and what doesn't)

**What works:**
- **Language & spelling:** Keep scripts, titles, and descriptions in US English (color, not colour; traveled, not travelled) — matches the biggest English-speaking, highest-RPM audience pool.
- **Topic mix:** RPM is driven by *viewer* geography and advertiser category demand, not creator location. Weaving in occasional US-specific stories (D.B. Cooper, Roanoke — already in your topic bank) tends to pull more US search traffic than an all-non-US lineup.
- **Video length for ad slots:** Longer videos (8+ minutes) unlock multiple mid-roll ad breaks once you're in YPP, meaningfully raising RPM per view. Episode 1 came in at 7:17 — fine for launch, but **aim for 9–12 minutes on future scripts** so mid-rolls become available.
- **Advertiser-friendly tone:** No profanity, no graphic descriptions of violence/death — the calm, respectful tone we've already built in is good for RPM, not just ethics.
- **Metadata in English, keyword-rich titles/descriptions/tags** — helps English-language search and suggested-video matching skew toward English-speaking (often higher-RPM) regions.
- **Chapters/timestamps in the description** — helps retention and session watch time, which indirectly supports both algorithmic reach and RPM.

**What does NOT work / don't do this:**
- **Do not set your AdSense account's country to the US or misrepresent your location.** That field is tied to tax and payment compliance and must match reality (Nigeria) — this isn't a targeting lever, it's a legal/financial detail. RPM is about *who watches*, not where you, the creator, are registered. Trying to fake this risks your AdSense account entirely.
- YouTube doesn't offer a "target monetization to country X" toggle for organic content — you influence viewer geography indirectly through language, topic, and timing, not by declaring a fake target country anywhere in Studio.

## Channel algorithm "warm-up" checklist
- Publish consistently on the same day(s)/time — predictability helps YouTube's recommender learn your pattern faster than sporadic uploads.
- Use Shorts to warm up first (above) — lower risk, faster feedback loop, and Shorts get broader cold-start discovery than long-form on a zero-subscriber channel.
- Don't edit titles/thumbnails or delete videos in the first 48 hours after publishing — it resets/confuses the early distribution signal.
- Respond to early comments within the first few hours; pin one comment per video (e.g., a follow-up fact or a question) to nudge engagement.
- Add each long-form into a single running playlist ("The Last Lantern — All Stories") to raise session watch time via autoplay-into-next-episode behavior.
- In every Short's pinned comment/caption, point back to the full episode — this is also how you convert Shorts virality into actual long-form watch hours over time, not just Shorts-view counts.
- Keep the channel in one lane (per the original plan's guardrails) — don't post unrelated content types even occasionally; consistency of format/niche is itself a trust signal both to viewers and to the inauthentic-content review process.
