# THE LAST LANTERN — CHANNEL MASTER ROADMAP & MONTHLY PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT

## PURPOSE
Build The Last Lantern into a distinctive, monetizable documentary channel centered on forgotten stories, unresolved mysteries, abandoned places and atmospheric sleep-story retellings.

The channel must prioritize originality, narrative quality, visual identity and sustainable production rather than maximum AI output.

## I. CHANNEL POSITIONING
**Core promise:** Quiet, cinematic documentaries about stories that history left behind.

**Recurring narrator: The Keeper.** Not a loud host — feels like an anonymous archivist bringing one forgotten story into the light.

## II. CONTENT PILLARS
Rotate deliberately between:
1. **Unsolved disappearances** — ships, expeditions, people, unexplained vanishings
2. **Eerie/forgotten history** — real historical events with unusual, unsettling, or overlooked details
3. **Abandoned places** — real locations with documented histories, decline, abandonment, human stories
4. **Sleep-story retellings** — longer, softer, slower versions of suitable stories designed for nighttime listening

## III. MONTHLY OUTPUT TARGET
- **Primary:** 4 long-form episodes/month, 10–18 min each
- **Secondary:** 4–6 Shorts per long-form episode (16–24 Shorts/month)
- Do not sacrifice long-form quality to increase Short output

## IV. MONTHLY PROGRAMMING MODEL
Rotating structure (e.g. Week 1 disappearance, Week 2 forgotten history, Week 3 abandoned place, Week 4 strongest available topic) — order changes when a stronger timely/strategic story appears. Do not force weak topics to satisfy the calendar.

## V. TOPIC SELECTION SYSTEM
Every candidate topic scored 1–10 on: curiosity, narrative depth, visual potential, historical credibility, emotional tension, search/discovery potential, thumbnail potential, Shorts potential, differentiation, subscription potential. Only strong candidates enter the production queue.

Priority to stories with: a powerful unanswered question, a strange documented detail, strong visual identity, enough material for 10–18 min, multiple independent Short opportunities, a memorable ending.

## VI. MONTHLY EPISODE DELIVERABLE
Every selected episode must have: Title, 3–5 alternative titles, central question, narrative angle, 5 hook options + hook score + final hook, full script, fact/theory map, scene list, image prompts, Flow motion plan, audio plan, thumbnail brief, Shorts plan, QC checklist.

## VII. EPISODE STRUCTURE
- **0:00–0:05:** Cold-open hook. No logo, no channel intro, no "welcome back," no unnecessary setup.
- **0:05 onward:** Transition into context → Setup (who/where/when/why care) → Escalation (sequential discoveries) → Climax (strangest/most important revelation) → Resolution (clearly separate KNOWN / LIKELY / DISPUTED / THEORY) → Closing (quiet emotional beat) → Outro (master lantern outro) → CTA (short, with reusable Subscribe asset)

## VIII. VISUAL PRODUCTION STRATEGY
Most scenes remain high-quality still images with controlled editorial movement. Flow motion reserved for ~2–3 high-value moments per episode, priority: (1) cold open, (2) major reveal, (3) emotional climax. Every Flow animation originates from an approved still whenever practical. Never spend Flow credits simply because motion is available.

## IX. MONTHLY FLOW CREDIT STRATEGY
*Note: the ~200 credits/month figure is per the user's plan as stated — not independently verifiable from this end; confirm directly in-account before budgeting production around it.*

Default target: 2–3 hero motion generations per long-form episode. Use cheaper generation modes for secondary motion where appropriate. Avoid repeatedly regenerating visually similar clips. Maintain a monthly contingency reserve for failed generations. If a motion clip has obvious AI artifacts, replace with the approved still rather than spending unlimited credits hiding the problem.

## X. PRODUCTION PIPELINE
1. **Research** (Gemini) — source-backed research dossiers
2. **Showrunning** (Claude) — topic, angle, hook, script, production structure
3. **Base production** (Arena/this agent) — Keeper narration, static imagery, captions, initial edit, ambient audio
4. **Motion** (Flow, run by the user) — animates only selected approved stills
5. **Final assembly** (Arena/this agent) — integrates Flow motion, narration, captions, ambient audio, logo, outro, CTA, Subscribe SVG, Bell SVG
6. **Thumbnail** — dedicated custom high-CTR composition based on the episode's strongest curiosity mechanism
7. **Quality control** — first 30 sec, hook, visual continuity, factual clarity, narration, captions, motion artifacts, ending, thumbnail/title relationship
8. **Publish** — video, separate thumbnail, title, description, chapters, tags/metadata, altered/synthetic content disclosure where required

## XI. SHORTS SYSTEM
4–6 Shorts per episode: (1) The Hook, (2) The Strangest Fact, (3) The Theory, (4) The Human Story, (5) The Visual Mystery, (6) The Full-Story Bridge. Every Short needs: hook, core information, escalation, payoff or open loop, CTA. Never turn Shorts into unrelated content for volume.

## XII. TITLE + THUMBNAIL SYSTEM
Title establishes the premise; thumbnail creates the visual curiosity gap. Don't repeat the same information in both.
*Example: Title "The Ghost Ship That Wouldn't Disappear" / Thumbnail "STILL AFLOAT?"*

## XIII. BRAND SYSTEM
Dark near-black base, one accent color per episode, cinematic lighting, amber lantern motif, premium illustrated/photographic composite feel, controlled negative space, restrained typography.

**Category accents:** Disappearances = cold blue-black · Forgotten history = deep red-black · Abandoned places = forest green-black · Sleep stories = muted purple-black. Consistent logo placement across all thumbnails.

## XIV. BRAND ASSETS (permanent, reusable — don't regenerate unnecessarily)
logo, icon, subscribe SVG, bell SVG, main_outro.mp4, cta.mp4, Keeper voice profile, caption style, thumbnail template/system, font system.

*Technical note: our current logo is a detailed painterly AI-generated PNG (gradients/texture), not a true flat vector — it can't become an actual `.svg` without a redesign into a simplified flat mark, since painterly illustration and vector format don't really coexist. Treating "logo" as the reusable asset regardless of file format unless/until a genuine flat-vector redesign is requested.*

## XV. MONETIZATION QUALITY STANDARD
Every episode should demonstrate: original narrative treatment, meaningful research, deliberate editing, custom visual storytelling, coherent audio design, distinctive branding, human-directed creative decisions, meaningful variation between episodes. Avoid mass-produced templates where only the subject name changes. The audience should feel each episode was individually made.

## XVI. MONTHLY REVIEW
Review: CTR, average view duration, retention curve, first-30-second retention, subscribers/video, Shorts→long-form conversion, comments/questions, topic/thumbnail/hook performance. Use findings to adjust next month's topics, hooks, titles, thumbnails, pacing, Shorts, visual strategy. Don't blindly repeat a format just because it was used before.

## XVII. SYSTEM IMPROVEMENT RULE
Any of Gemini/Claude/Arena/Flow may propose process improvements, but must propose before implementing a major change, using this format:
**Problem / Proposed change / Expected benefit / Cost-resource impact / Risk / Recommendation.**
The system should continuously improve rather than staying locked to its first workflow.

## XVIII. FINAL CREATIVE PRINCIPLE
The Last Lantern should never feel like "an AI made another documentary." It should feel like: *"Someone discovered a forgotten story, researched it deeply, reconstructed it beautifully, and brought it to me by lantern light."* Technology disappears behind the storytelling. The audience remembers the story, the atmosphere, the Keeper, the question — not the AI tools used to make it.
