# Fast-Monetization Channel Plan v2 — Narrated Sleep/Mystery History Channel
### (Revised for the 2026 "Inauthentic Content" enforcement environment)

> **What changed from v1 and why:** YouTube's July 2025 "inauthentic content" policy (the renamed "repetitious content" rule) is being enforced aggressively in 2026 — 16 faceless/voiceover channels with 35M combined subs were terminated in January 2026 alone. The exact pattern being targeted is: TTS narration + stock/AI imagery + a fixed weekly template with no visible authorship. v1's topic-rotation guardrail helps but isn't enough on its own. v2 keeps the entire production system (same tools, same rhythm, same timeline) and layers in the specific fixes needed to read as *authored*, not *templated* — plus disclosure, fact-check, licensing, and workload safeguards. Nothing about the schedule got harder; the additions are mostly one-time setup + small weekly habits.

---

## Reality check (don't skip)
- Full ad-revenue YPP = 1,000 subs + (4,000 watch hours/12mo OR 10M Shorts views/90 days).
- Lower "Expanded" tier = 500 subs + 3 public uploads/90 days + (3,000 watch hours OR 3M Shorts views)/90 days — fan funding only, not ads.
- **Realistic 2-month target: hit the Expanded tier (near-certain with consistent Shorts output) and land in genuine striking distance of full YPP if a Short breaks out.** Full ad-revenue YPP in 2 months is possible but partly depends on a video actually going viral — no schedule guarantees that.
- **New in v2:** Monetization eligibility is judged **channel-wide**. A batch of videos that reads as templated can pull ad eligibility from your entire catalog, not just those uploads. This makes the authorship/disclosure steps below load-bearing, not optional polish.

## Niche
**Calm narrated true stories: unsolved mysteries, eerie/forgotten history, "vanished without a trace" style stories — packaged as sleep/relaxation content, told by a consistent named narrator persona.**

Sub-pillars to rotate (unchanged from v1, still useful against topical sameness):
1. Unsolved disappearances (ships, expeditions, people)
2. Forgotten historical events with an eerie edge
3. Abandoned places with a real backstory
4. "Sleep stories" — slower, longer-form versions of the above, explicitly framed for falling asleep to

**v2 addition — the channel needs a face, even if not a literal on-camera face:**
A recurring **named narrator persona** (e.g. a specific voice, a consistent host name/bio in the About page, a signature sign-off line, a consistent visual mark like a recurring intro card/logo sting) is now a core deliverable, not decoration. This is the single highest-leverage change for surviving the inauthentic-content review, because reviewers and the algorithm are both explicitly weighing "is there a recognizable authored point of view" vs. "is this interchangeable with 500 similar channels."

## Why this beats every option I could construct around Arena's real limits
- No recurring *character* needed for the visuals = no consistency problem for Arena's random-model battles; atmosphere/scenery shots don't need to "match" a previous shot.
- Same low-motion, narration-carried structure suits Arena's 3-clips/day cap.
- No COPPA ceiling — normal ad rates apply, **provided** the channel is correctly marked "not made for kids" and doesn't get auto-misclassified (see Guardrails).
- Natural short-form spin-off from every long-form script (biggest lever for hitting monetization thresholds fast).

## Tool stack (all free) — with a licensing check added
- **Claude** — script writing, hook writing, title/thumbnail copy, weekly planning, state tracking, **fact-check pass** (new, see below).
- **Arena** — 2–3 atmospheric video clips per week (fog rolling, candle flicker, water, old paper), banked into a growing reusable library.
- **Free image tool** — historical-style illustrations, maps, "still" scenes for the bulk of runtime.
- **Free TTS (or your own recorded voice)** — narration. A real human voice performs better here *and* is the single easiest way to strengthen the "authored, not templated" case — bank this as the top option if it's feasible at all.
- **Free music/SFX library** (YouTube Audio Library + free ambient SFX sites) — rain, wind, fire crackle, quiet piano.
- **CapCut** — assembly, pans/zooms on stills, auto-captions, cutting Shorts out of the long-form cut.

**v2 addition — one-time licensing check (Day 1, ~30 min, do this before banking any assets):**
For each free image/TTS/music tool you actually pick, confirm in its terms: (a) commercial/monetized-video use is allowed, (b) no mandatory on-screen attribution that YouTube TTS/image sites sometimes require, (c) output ownership/usage rights are clear (some free AI image tools license outputs non-exclusively or restrict resale/redistribution). Keep a one-line note per tool in your state-tracking doc so you're not re-checking mid-project.

## Weekly production rhythm (repeats every week of the 8 weeks)
- **1 long-form video/week**: 10–18 minutes, one full story, slow pace, calm narration, **narrator persona sign-on/sign-off intact**.
- **4–6 Shorts/week**: cut directly from that week's story (the hook, the eeriest single detail, the unresolved ending).
- **1 asset-banking session/week**: generate that week's 2–3 Arena atmosphere clips + still images, add to your reusable library.
- **v2 addition — 15-minute fact-check + disclosure pass/week** (folds into the existing script day, not a new day): see Guardrails below.
- **v2 addition — built-in slack:** treat each week as a 6-day plan with 1 float day. If a week's Shorts count slips to 3 instead of 5, the float day is your catch-up, not a scramble. This doesn't change the total 8-week arc, it just stops one bad week from compounding into a missed threshold.

## 8-week day-by-day plan

**Week 1 — Setup**
- Day 1: Name channel, build simple logo/banner (free image tool), write channel description, set up AdSense-ready Google account, enable 2-Step Verification. **Define the narrator persona now**: name, one-paragraph "voice" bio for the About page, a consistent sign-off line for every video (e.g., "...and that's where the trail goes cold. Sleep well."). Do the tool licensing check (30 min, see above).
- Day 2: In upload defaults, set **"Not Made for Kids"** explicitly, and review YouTube Studio's "Altered or synthetic content" disclosure setting so it's a reflex, not an afterthought (see Guardrails).
- Day 3: Claude drafts Story #1 script + a bank of 10 future story topics. **Add the fact-check step here** (below) before the script is considered final.
- Day 4–5: Generate the first atmosphere/still asset library (10–15 reusable images/clips) — standing visual backbone for weeks to come.
- Day 6: Record/generate narration for Story #1.
- Day 7: Assemble in CapCut (stills + pans + narration + music + captions + persona sign-off), export long-form + cut 4 Shorts. Upload long-form + first Short, applying the synthetic-content disclosure toggle where relevant.

**Weeks 2–4 — Consistency + Shorts volume**
- Same weekly rhythm. Priority: never miss the weekly long-form, never drop below 4 Shorts/week.
- Every script keeps the persona voice and sign-off consistent; vary *structure* (which detail leads, where the twist lands, pacing) so episodes aren't swappable with each other — this is the actual test reviewers apply.
- End of Week 4: review Studio analytics with Claude — which topics/hooks are outperforming — double down on that sub-pillar for weeks 5–8. **Also check the "Content" tab in Studio for any policy notices** — catching an early inauthentic-content warning in week 4 is recoverable; catching it in week 8 is not.

**Weeks 5–8 — Push toward thresholds**
- Increase Shorts to 5–7/week using the growing asset library.
- Keep long-form weekly, but tighten titles/thumbnails/hooks based on Week 1–4 data.
- Around Week 6–7: check YouTube Studio's monetization tab for live progress toward both thresholds. Apply for Expanded (fan funding) as soon as thresholds are hit; don't wait for full YPP.
- Week 8: reassess honestly — Expanded tier only is a legitimate on-schedule outcome; full YPP becomes a realistic Month 3–4 target.

## Guardrails (protects the monetization application itself — expanded in v2)

**Carried over from v1:**
- Vary story structure/pacing across episodes — don't let every video look template-identical.
- No fake clickbait ("you won't believe...") without payoff — thumbnails/titles must match content.
- Keep a visible narrative voice/personality across scripts.

**New in v2 — these directly target the 2026 enforcement pattern:**

1. **Authorship signal, every video.** Consistent persona name/bio, consistent sign-off, an occasional first-person editorial aside in the script ("historians still argue about this next part" / "here's the detail that convinced me this one wasn't an accident") — small, cheap additions that make the channel read as *someone's* work rather than an auto-pipeline output.
2. **Synthetic content disclosure.** If narration/imagery realistically depicts real people, places, or events in a way a viewer could mistake for authentic footage, toggle "Altered or synthetic content" at upload. It costs nothing and removes the 3-strike disclosure risk (warning → 90-day monetization suspension → removal) entirely.
3. **Fact-check pass before a script is "done."** Have Claude do a second pass specifically listing every factual claim about real people/places/events in the script, and spot-check the ones about identifiable real individuals or their families (names, cause of death, fate) against a real source before publishing. This protects against hallucinated details and reduces reputational/defamation exposure — especially for cases involving real missing persons with living family members.
4. **Tone check for real-tragedy material.** For stories involving real deaths/disappearances with identifiable people, keep language respectful rather than exploitative — "packaged for sleep" should mean *calming delivery*, not *trivializing the subject*. Avoid dramatized dialogue attributed to real people that isn't documented.
5. **"Not Made for Kids" stays explicit every upload.** Soft/calm content can trip the "made for kids" classifier; leaving it on Studio's default per video (rather than a one-time channel setting) avoids ad/comment suppression on individual videos.
6. **Licensing note per asset source**, logged once in Week 1, referenced instead of re-litigated each week.
7. **Slack day per week** so a shortfall doesn't cascade — protects the *volume* consistency the thresholds actually require.

## One-time state-tracking checklist (Claude maintains this, update weekly)
- [ ] Persona name, bio, sign-off line locked
- [ ] Tool licensing notes (image / TTS / music) — confirmed commercial-use safe
- [ ] "Not Made for Kids" set per upload
- [ ] Synthetic-content disclosure applied where relevant, per upload
- [ ] Fact-check pass completed per script
- [ ] Weekly output: 1 long-form / 4–6 Shorts / 1 asset session — logged with float-day usage
- [ ] Studio "Content" tab checked monthly for any policy notices
- [ ] Subs / watch hours / Shorts views tracked against both thresholds
